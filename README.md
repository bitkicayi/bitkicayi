## 🧠 about me

i'm studying computer engineering at Bursa Technical University, but to be completely honest, most of my days are spent having existential crises in front of a messy heap of wires, sensors, and randomly blinking LEDs on my desk. my answer to the absolute meaninglessness of the universe is building embedded systems that run purely on voltage and stubbornness: Raspberry Pi, STM32, ESP32, and other tiny pieces of silicon that somehow manage to make me question my own intelligence.

currently, i'm doing a **long-term internship**, getting my hands dirty with embedded systems, hardware integration, and all the delightful ways electronics can refuse to cooperate. a good portion of my work involves **C++, firmware development, and web interfaces that make embedded devices slightly less miserable to interact with**. naturally, there's also an AI assistant involved, confidently generating code while i confidently pretend to understand all of it. somewhere between **CAN bus, RS-485, UART, MQTT**, and the occasional missing common ground, i've learned that debugging is less of a technical process and more of a philosophical confrontation with reality.

lately, i've been growing increasingly interested in **Embedded Linux, device drivers, and low-level systems programming**. i'd like to eventually understand what's actually happening beneath all those layers of abstraction instead of just celebrating when something mysteriously starts working. the dream is to get comfortable enough with **C, Linux internals, and hardware-software interactions** that i can confidently break things at a much lower level. progress is slow, the rabbit hole is deep, and the kernel doesn't particularly care about my feelings.

every now and then, i wander into backend development, networking, and whatever else catches my attention. i also hold a **Cisco CCNAv7 ITN certification**, which, unfortunately, has never convinced an uncooperative MQTT broker to reconsider its life choices.

**in short:** the loops are infinite, the documentation is occasionally fictional, and half the code probably works for reasons nobody fully understands. the universe remains profoundly indifferent to whether my firmware compiles, but as long as there's fresh filter coffee on the desk and another piece of hardware refusing to cooperate, i suppose there's still a reason to get up tomorrow.
